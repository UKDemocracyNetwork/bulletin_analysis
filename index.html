    How to read this map

    Each dot is one item from the UK Democracy Bulletin: a story, note, caption or list entry. Each bubble is a topic group, and an item sits in the group it is mainly about.

    Distance from the centre is relevance. The centre of each bubble is 100% relevant to its topic group, and the outer ring is 0%. The faint rings mark 75%, 50% and 25%. The scale is the same in every bubble, so you can compare distances across groups. Where many items share a score they push each other apart into a band, and where no items have a score you see a gap. The shape comes from the data, and the angle around the centre means nothing.

    Relevance starts from the classifier's confidence (Core 100%, Arguable 60%, Stretch 20%). It loses 10 points if a boundary rule had to decide the item, and 8 points for each other topic group it touches (up to two). The dot style shows the confidence:

    Core
    Solid. The classifier was confident about the topic group.
    Arguable
    Light. There's a case for the group, but it could be argued.
    Stretch
    Hollow. The group's boundary rules didn't really settle it.

    The scores come in a few steps rather than a smooth range, which is why dots gather in bands.

    Out-of-scope items are the ones the classifier ruled are not democracy stories, such as jobs, events and funding calls. They're hidden until you switch them on.
